# Evidence for selecting a stronger standard prior

Audited 2026-10-03. This note revisits the supplied papers themselves, using the extracted PDF text in `papers/extracted/`, and checks the linked primary publications. It distinguishes controlled prior experiments from descriptions of competitive systems. It does not report a new pretrained model or establish an optimal mixture.

## What changes the recommendation

The current `R_P1_05` recipe is a replication control with a categorical extension. It should remain reproducible, but should no longer be presented as the conclusion of the search for the strongest standard prior. The evidence supports a more demanding challenger: preserve a substantial compositional-SCM core, explicitly vary the observed-data process and finite-context difficulty, test complementary direct tree and smooth mechanisms, and measure whether typed categorical structure survives the generator-to-model interface. Finance continuation is a separate objective and cannot supply missing coverage in the retained standard checkpoint.

There is no literature-supported fixed percentage allocation that can be called optimal. The strongest defensible outcome without the cluster is an explicit initial challenger, a small contrastive experiment design, generator diagnostics that can reject bad candidates cheaply, and a rule for choosing the final mixture from real development performance. Correlation matching or a synthetic diversity score alone cannot replace that selection.

## What frontier descriptions establish

**TabPFN-3.5:** Section 3.3 of the supplied report says the generator follows TabPFN-3, takes inspiration from TabICLv2, and targets high-cardinality, wide and grouped tables. The same release changes the cell representation, model width, and joint classification/regression training. This supports a prior–representation research program; it does not isolate a secret winning prior mixture. The report's headline encompasses different family members and inference procedures, so reproduce the named checkpoint and budget when comparing. [Primary report](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.17895v2#S3.SS3)

**Kumo Tabular:** The supplied release article describes compositional latent graphs, different missingness patterns, coarsened inputs with conflicting labels, high-cardinality categories and heavy-tailed regression. It also changes architecture, training scale and context curriculum. Those capabilities are sensible parity requirements, but the article does not provide component ablations or enough generator probabilities to reconstruct a unique optimal distribution. At the audited snapshot, full pretraining reproducibility is not established by the public inference repository and weights. [Release article](https://huggingface.co/blog/nvidia/kumo-tabular), [official repository](https://github.com/NVIDIA/structured-data-models)

**Xiaomi and EXAONE:** The supplied reports describe broader functions or observation transformations, but do not isolate their marginal benefit against the same backbone and training budget. Xiaomi’s task-adaptive inference and EXAONE’s retained cell representation are additional explanations for performance. They justify separate architecture and inference controls, not attribution of all gains to synthetic priors. [Xiaomi paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03880), [EXAONE paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25774)

No missing detail in a public report is evidence that the authors omitted the corresponding operation internally. Likewise, “released model” does not mean “released exact generator and training recipe.” Current repositories and checkpoint revisions should be checked again immediately before benchmark runs.

## Adaptive priors: useful precedent and limitations

Robust Tabular Foundation Models, Equations 3–8, adapts generator settings according to a model's loss relative to fitted baselines, with an entropy constraint that yields softmax weights in the active-constraint case. Its reported TabPFNv2 results support targeted adaptation as a candidate. The study uses MLP SCMs and does not establish superiority against the 2026 frontier. [Primary paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.03307v1)

For our experiments, a baseline-relative gap is a finite-sample learning signal, not an exact Bayes regret. Each reference learner must see the same support features and labels as the TFM. Tune it on a separate calibration split, then estimate the gap on independent query rows. Taking the minimum loss across many learners on the same query batch otherwise makes the selected baseline optimistically biased. Include both tree and smooth reference learners: using only ExtraTrees can hide smooth problems it cannot solve. Preserve fixed replay mass so adaptation does not erase well-covered mechanisms.

A KL-regularized update against a nonzero reference mixture gives `q_i ∝ w_i exp(g_i / tau)` directly; it is preferable to claiming that a fixed entropy constraint always has a unique positive solution. With tied maximum gaps and a sufficiently loose entropy constraint, the latter claim has boundary exceptions. Adapt only after the fixed challenger demonstrates positive transfer; hard-but-irreducible problems must not receive mass merely because their raw loss is large.

## Candidate axes that the evidence justifies testing

These are proposed interventions, not assertions of novelty or established improvements.

1. **Observed-data coverage.** Independently switch between clean, independently missing, feature-dependent missing, value-dependent missing and coarsened measurements. Generate labels from the underlying mechanism before observing or coarsening features. Retain the mask; distinguish latent-information loss from additive label noise. Keep a substantial clean component. Classify missingness relative to the generated full data, not merely by the name of a masking function.
2. **Typed categorical inference.** Test long-tailed category counts, observed shared groups, random intercepts/slopes, and unseen groups, preserving category type end-to-end. Compare against an otherwise matched random-relabeling control. Unobserved hierarchy cannot supply identifiable similarity between arbitrary unseen category names; a shared group identifier or covariate structure is necessary for that transfer claim.
3. **Context-conditioned complexity.** Vary active dimension, interaction order, local neighborhood size, occupied leaves and category support counts jointly with context size. Preserve underdetermined tasks as a controlled fraction. A scalar acceptance test that rejects every hard task would defeat this design.
4. **Complementary prediction geometry.** Compare direct tree/boosted-residual tasks, sparse smooth additive/interacting tasks, local mixtures and graph-composed mechanisms. Match observed SNR, shape and category/missingness distribution when asking whether mechanism families are complementary. Otherwise a mixture comparison conflates these factors.
5. **Filter sensitivity.** Compare native reference filtering with a permissive challenger filter that removes numerical invalidity but stratifies by estimated finite-context attainability. Do not simply turn filtering off: TabICLv2 provides evidence that its native filter helps convergence. Use a separate diagnostic stream to characterize accepted versus rejected tasks before changing the training distribution.

The standard candidate needs these axes for ordinary classification and regression. Delayed verification, finite populations and context acquisition remain specialist dimensions for fraud. They should not consume large standard-training mass by default.

## Selection without wasting the main allocation

The literature supports the following sequence; specific budgets and weight vectors belong in the experiment specification rather than being inferred from leaderboard positions.

- Preserve native R and the existing R+P1 recipe as controls. Record the exact generator revision, realized accepted-task counts, shape distribution, generation cost and encoded feature types.
- Use CPU diagnostics to reject broken or redundant generators: invariance tests, support/query leakage checks, occupancy coverage, conditional-noise checks, finite-context tree/smooth teacher performance, and matched counterfactual cases that differ in one mechanism. These are structural checks, not pretrained-model performance estimates.
- Screen a small number of coherent challengers and one-factor interventions with a common architecture and training budget. Do not tune on benchmark test folds. Use source-disjoint development families and reserve an untouched confirmation set, with duplicate or derived datasets grouped together.
- Promote the leading priors to a second model size and a cell-preserving architecture control. Fix the inference procedure during prior comparisons; then measure the accuracy–latency frontier separately.
- Select mixture mass by paired development gains with uncertainty and interference penalties. If two additions help alone but hurt together, test their interaction rather than averaging their scores. Use at least two fresh seeds for promotion and a third when the apparent improvement is small or inconsistent. A final architecture–prior pair needs a confirmatory run at the chosen scale.

The mechanism name count is not the objective. The useful quantity is additional transferable predictive skill per unit of training and inference cost, subject to preserving existing strengths. This evidence warrants a stronger challenger and a reproducible selection procedure; it does not warrant another fixed mixture presented as already optimal.
